China's EVs Prove More Reliable at High Mileage

Sep 24, 2026 2 min read 👁

A comprehensive analysis of 47.4 million UK vehicle inspection records has shown a surprising advantage for electric vehicles as mileage increases. When cars reach 90,000-120,000 miles, electric vehicles show a 25% lower failure rate compared to their gasoline counterparts, with 16.5% versus 22.1% failing annual inspections. This finding challenges conventional wisdom about long-term reliability of electric cars and could influence consumer purchasing decisions globally.

The data reveals that the reliability gap widens even further for vehicles exceeding 120,000 miles, with electric cars maintaining a 16% failure rate compared to 23.5% for gasoline vehicles. These statistics come from the UK’s Ministry of Transport (MOT) testing system, which conducts mandatory annual safety inspections for vehicles over three years old. The study analyzed records from both electric and conventional vehicles across different makes and models.

Interestingly, the reliability difference between electric and gasoline vehicles is less pronounced at lower mileage points. This suggests that while electric cars may have higher initial costs, they potentially offer better long-term value for drivers who keep their vehicles for extended periods or high-mileage usage. The findings are particularly relevant as the global automotive market transitions toward electrification.

However, the research also identified specific challenges for electric vehicles. Electric car tires wear out approximately twice as fast as those on gasoline-powered vehicles, likely due to the instant torque delivery characteristic of electric motors. This represents an additional maintenance cost that potential EV buyers should consider when evaluating total ownership costs.

The study did not examine battery health during inspections, which remains a significant concern for many potential electric vehicle buyers. Battery degradation over time can affect range and performance, though modern EV batteries typically come with warranties covering eight years or 100,000 miles. As battery technology continues to improve, this factor may become less of a concern in the coming years.

China, as the world’s largest producer and market for electric vehicles, has been rapidly expanding its EV production capabilities. Chinese manufacturers like BYD, NIO, and XPeng have been making significant inroads into international markets, offering competitively priced electric vehicles with increasingly advanced features. The positive reliability data from the UK could further bolster the appeal of Chinese electric vehicles in global markets.

As automotive technology evolves, this research provides valuable insights for consumers, manufacturers, and policymakers. The data suggests that electric vehicles may offer superior long-term reliability, potentially reducing the total cost of ownership despite higher initial purchase prices. With many countries implementing policies to reduce emissions, this information could accelerate the global transition to electric mobility.
