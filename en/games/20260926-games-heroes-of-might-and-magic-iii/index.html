Heroes of Might and Magic III Returns: A 27-Year-Old Strategy Classic Gets a 3D Remake

Sep 26, 2026 3 min read 👁

At gamescom 2026’s Opening Night Live, Ubisoft confirmed one of the worst-kept secrets in strategy gaming: Heroes of Might and Magic III is coming back. The 1999 turn-based classic will be rebuilt in full 3D and 4K, keeping the original game and both of its expansions intact, with a release planned for 2027 on PC, PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. For a game that first shipped on Windows 98, the announcement landed like a reunion rather than a reboot.

For the uninitiated, Heroes of Might and Magic III — usually shortened to Heroes III — is a turn-based strategy game set in a fantasy world of castles, dragons and necromancers. Players explore a map with hero characters, gather resources, build up towns and then fight tactical battles on a hex grid. It is slow, deliberate and endlessly replayable, and it is the reason the phrase “just one more turn” exists.

Its reputation has aged unusually well. While later entries in the series came and went, Heroes III never really left. A passionate modding community kept it alive for decades, building new campaigns, balance patches and quality-of-life fixes, and the game still appears on best-of-all-time lists compiled by outlets far younger than it is. In China, it occupies a particularly warm place in PC gaming memory, where it was a staple of internet cafes and home computers in the 2000s and remains a frequent topic in strategy forums today.

That long afterlife is exactly what makes a remake tricky. Fans do not simply want prettier graphics; they want the same game, with the same quirks, the same balance and the same map editor. Ubisoft’s promise of a “full 3D, 4K” treatment suggests the visuals will change dramatically, but the company has been careful to say the original content and both expansions will be preserved. Whether that means a faithful remaster with a new engine or something closer to a reimagining is the question every veteran is asking.

The timing also reflects a broader trend. Nostalgia has become one of the safest bets in the games industry, as publishers look for proven properties in a market where new blockbusters cost hundreds of millions and can still flop. Remakes of beloved strategy and role-playing games offer a familiar audience, a built-in marketing hook and a much smaller creative risk. Heroes III, with its decades of goodwill, fits that formula almost perfectly.

For newcomers, the appeal may be harder to see at first glance. There are no cinematic set pieces or voice-acted cutscenes here, just a map, a budget and a growing army. But that simplicity is the point. Heroes III rewards planning, patience and a willingness to learn from a disastrous first attempt, and its best moments are the ones players create themselves — a desperate siege won with a handful of troops, a hero who somehow survives a fight they had no business winning.

A 2027 release is still far enough away that details remain thin. What is clear is that a game older than many of its potential players is being handed a rare second life, and that a community which never stopped playing is about to be asked to fall in love with it all over again. Whether the remake becomes the definitive version or simply sends everyone back to the 1999 original, the announcement has already done one thing: it got people talking about Heroes III again.
