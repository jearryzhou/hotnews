China's Indie and AAA Studios Bet Big on Global Audiences

Sep 26, 2026 3 min read 👁

Chinese game studios are having a busy autumn, rolling out new titles across almost every genre and platform. Much of the attention is on PC storefront Steam, where several China-developed games have just announced release dates or playable demos, signaling a maturing industry that is no longer content with mobile-only hits.

One of the more unusual projects is No Such Place, a top-down shooter with extraction mechanics from Lianghe Games’ Crit team. Set in fractured dimensional shards, players join a paranormal investigation outfit called U.R.C., scavenge for supplies and clues, and race a timer to reach an extraction point. The game launches in early access on October 14. It is a distinctly Chinese take on the “extraction shooter” genre popularized by Western hits, swapping military realism for sci-fi mystery.

On a very different note, Cybernetic Walrus has announced Rip It! Card Collector, a tactile simulation game about the addictive ritual of opening trading card packs. Players tear, sort and organize thousands of virtual cards, chasing rare pulls. The studio previously made Order 13 and Roadside Research, and the new title is heading to PC with Chinese-language support. It reflects a global trend: games about collecting and sorting everyday objects have found surprisingly large audiences.

Bigger budgets are in play too. Annihilation Tide, a Chinese AAA action game backed by Tencent, recently held its first large-scale offline demo event in Chengdu, drawing more than a thousand players over two days. The venue was modeled on the British Museum’s round reading room, with a sword-in-the-stone centerpiece, blending Arthurian legend with modern London. The developers say they are deliberately targeting a niche: fast, high-skill combat rather than the slower, heavier style of many action role-playing games.

At gamescom 2026’s Opening Night Live, miHoYo opened the show by revealing a new project, Primordial Knot, while NetEase’s Infinite Infinity got a release window. The event underlined how Chinese publishers now treat global showcases as core marketing moments rather than afterthoughts. Meanwhile, Tencent’s TiMi studio confirmed that King of Glory: Chess of All Things, a strategy spinoff of its massive Honor of Kings franchise, will launch in September after surpassing 50 million pre-registrations.

Long-running titles are evolving as well. Sword Net 3, a martial arts online game that first launched 17 years ago, held a fan festival in Nansha, near Guangzhou, where players in faction costumes filled a sports center hours before the show began. The game remains a cultural touchstone for a generation of Chinese players, and its developer is betting that community events can keep an aging online world vibrant.

International crossovers are another growth strategy. Sloclap’s football game Rematch launched its fifth season alongside a collaboration with Blue Lock, the popular soccer anime, running from September 24 to October 23 with a new limited mode, a free event pass and character bundles inspired by the show’s stars. Sega, meanwhile, used Tokyo Game Show 2026 to discuss Sonic Racing: CrossWorlds and its second season, which includes crossovers with Godzilla and Evangelion, as the Sonic series celebrates its 35th anniversary.

Taken together, the slate suggests Chinese developers are increasingly comfortable competing on craft and genre rather than scale alone. Whether these bets pay off will depend on global players, but the ambition is unmistakable.
